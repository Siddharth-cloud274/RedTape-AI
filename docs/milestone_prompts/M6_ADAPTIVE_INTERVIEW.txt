PHASE C — CONTEXT-AWARE INTERVIEW PLANNING
==================================================

The interview engine must determine:

1. The user's current administrative goal.
2. Which facts are already known.
3. Whether the intent remains ambiguous.
4. Which preliminary context is missing.
5. Whether previous facts are contradictory.
6. Whether additional questions are useful.
7. Whether the conversation should pause.
8. Whether enough context exists for future
   official-source research.

Use only preliminary context schemas.

Examples of possible context:

INCOME CERTIFICATE:
- State
- District
- Purpose

DOMICILE CERTIFICATE:
- State
- District
- Purpose

TRADE LICENCE:
- Business location
- Business type
- New application or renewal

FSSAI REGISTRATION:
- Food business type
- State or business location
- New application or renewal

FOOD BUSINESS SETUP:
- Proposed business type
- Intended business location
- Whether the business already operates

These are provisional context examples.

They are NOT official government checklists.

Do not assert that these are legally required
fields without official verification.

Question requirements must remain controlled
by backend schemas rather than invented by Gemini.

==================================================
PHASE D — INTELLIGENT QUESTION SELECTION
==================================================

Implement an adaptive next-question planner.

Requirements:

1. Use validated known facts.
2. Identify unresolved context.
3. Prioritize high-value clarification questions.
4. Ask one primary question at a time.
5. Avoid repeating answered questions.
6. Avoid irrelevant questions.
7. Understand answers provided out of order.
8. Recognize multiple facts in one message.
9. Support corrections to previous answers.
10. Respect user refusals and skipped answers.
11. Do not repeatedly ask the same skipped question.
12. Support natural changes in conversation topic.

The question planner must be deterministic
where possible.

Gemini may generate natural wording, but
the backend chooses which fields need attention.

Do not use a rigid hardcoded sequence of
questions for every conversation.

EXAMPLE:

User:
I need an income certificate for college.
I'm from Kolkata, West Bengal.

Expected:
Extract the goal, purpose, state and district.

Do not ask again for those known facts.

The planner should determine if another
preliminary clarification is necessary.

PHASE E — STRUCTURED INTERVIEW DECISIONS
==================================================

Use Pydantic to validate interview decisions.

Suggested structure:

{
  "interview_status": "collecting_context",
  "current_intent": "trade_licence",
  "known_fields": [
    "business_type",
    "business_location"
  ],
  "missing_context": [
    "application_type"
  ],
  "next_action": "ask_question",
  "target_field": "application_type",
  "question": "Is this a new application or a renewal?"
}

Supported interview statuses:

- understanding_goal
- collecting_context
- clarification_needed
- paused
- ready_for_research
- unsupported

Supported next actions:

- ask_question
- clarify_conflict
- acknowledge_information
- pause_interview
- explain_limitation
- handoff_to_future_research

The backend must validate:

- Status and next-action compatibility.
- Target field belongs to the active schema.
- Already-known confirmed facts are not
  unnecessarily requested.
- Questions are relevant to the current intent.
- Unsupported workflows are not treated as
  fully available.
- Model output cannot bypass validation rules.

The model's output is a proposal.

The backend remains authoritative.

PHASE F — CONVERSATIONAL NATURALNESS
==================================================

RedTape AI must sound like a helpful assistant,
not like an application form.

Good response:

"Got it. Which municipality is your shop in?"

Bad response:

"Please complete Field 3: Municipality."

Avoid:

- Select option 1, 2 or 3.
- Choose your service from the menu.
- Please fill out all mandatory fields.
- Long lists of unrelated questions.
- Repetitive acknowledgments.
- Unnecessary formal language.

The assistant should:

1. Acknowledge new information briefly.
2. Ask the next useful question naturally.
3. Understand short replies such as:
   "Kolkata", "First time", "For college".
4. Use prior conversation context.
5. Understand messages containing several facts.
6. Respond meaningfully to corrections.
7. Avoid excessive follow-up questions.

Preserve the user's chosen language when practical.

Support ordinary English, Hindi and
mixed Hindi-English conversational messages
without changing the application's internal schema.

PHASE G — CORRECTIONS AND CONFLICTS
==================================================

Integrate with the existing M5 memory manager.

EXAMPLE 1:

User:
My shop is in Kolkata.

Later:
Actually, it's in Howrah.

Expected:
Update the business location to Howrah.
Invalidate dependent context if necessary.
Do not continue asking Kolkata-specific questions.

EXAMPLE 2:

User:
I live in Kolkata, but my father's shop
is in Barrackpore.

Expected:
Store residence location and business location
as distinct facts.

Do not overwrite one with the other.

EXAMPLE 3:

User:
I don't know which municipality it comes under.

Expected:
Do not repeatedly ask the same question.
Mark the information as unresolved.
Explain that the jurisdiction can be checked
during future official-source research.

Do not invent the municipality.

==================================================
PHASE H — INTERVIEW COMPLETION LOGIC
==================================================

Implement controlled stopping conditions.

The interview should pause or stop when:

1. Enough preliminary context has been collected.
2. The next information requires official research.
3. The user asks to pause.
4. The user declines to provide more information.
5. The intent is unsupported or unresolved.
6. The assistant cannot safely determine the
   next useful question.

Use ready_for_research only to indicate that
sufficient preliminary context is available.

It must NOT mean:

- Government eligibility confirmed.
- Application approved.
- Official required documents verified.
- Application is complete.
- Documents are ready for submission.

Example response:

"I have the basic details needed to look into
the applicable process. Official verification
is not enabled yet."

Do not claim that live research was performed.

Do not continue asking questions just to
increase the number of interview turns.
